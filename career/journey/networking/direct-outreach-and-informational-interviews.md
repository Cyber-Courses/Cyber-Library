# Direct Outreach and Informational Interviews

Direct outreach is the deliberate practice of contacting an individual security professional to learn about their work and, over time, to build a relationship. An **informational interview** is a short conversation whose explicit purpose is to gather information and perspective, **not to request a job**. Keeping that distinction clear is what makes the approach effective and welcome.

## Why it works in security

- Many security roles are filled through **referral and reputation** rather than open application, so a real relationship carries weight.
- A great deal of practical knowledge lives in the heads of practitioners rather than in formal curricula.
- A newcomer who speaks with an incident responder, a penetration tester, or a governance analyst learns what the daily work genuinely involves, which skills and certifications carry weight for that specialty, and how the person navigated their own path.
- These conversations surface the unwritten norms of a subfield, the tradeoffs between consulting and in-house work, and realities such as on-call rotations or client pressure that rarely appear in job descriptions.

## How to make the ask

- Keep the message short, specific, and **easy to say yes to**.
- Name why you are contacting this particular person, ask for a modest amount of time, and make the logistics simple.
- Do your homework first by reading the person's writing, talks, or public projects. It informs good questions and signals genuine interest.

## Running the conversation and following up

- Come prepared with thoughtful questions, respect the agreed time, and listen more than you speak.
- Send a prompt thank-you note, and later a brief update on how the advice was used. That turns a single exchange into an ongoing connection.
- As the relationship matures, offer value in return: share useful findings, make introductions, or follow the person's work with care. **Reciprocity**, not entitlement, sustains a network.

## How it fits with other networking

This practice complements the other avenues rather than replacing them. It is often most powerful when it grows from one of them: a talk attended at an [industry event](industry-events-and-conferences.md), a thread answered in an [online community](online-communities-and-forums.md), or a chapter met through a [professional organization](professional-organizations.md) gives outreach a legitimate starting point and makes a later message feel natural.
