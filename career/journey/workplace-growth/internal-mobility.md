# Internal Mobility

Internal mobility is the practice of moving into a new role, team, or specialization within the organization you already work for, rather than changing employers to do so. In security it covers **lateral moves** between functions, such as a security operations analyst shifting into threat intelligence or application security, as well as vertical moves into roles with broader scope. It is one of the most common ways security careers evolve, because the field spans many specialized disciplines that a single person can explore over time without leaving the same company.

## Why organizations and individuals favor it

- Security teams value people who already understand the organization's systems, risks, and culture; someone who has handled incidents on a company's infrastructure brings **context** an outside hire would need months to acquire.
- For the individual, an internal move preserves relationships, tenure, and accumulated trust while still offering the stretch of unfamiliar work.
- For the organization, it retains institutional knowledge and signals that growth is possible without an exit.

## How moves usually happen

- They begin informally, well before any role changes hands: expressing interest to a manager, volunteering for projects that touch the target area, or shadowing a neighboring team to build credibility.
- Many organizations formalize this through **internal job postings**, **rotation programs**, or **secondments**, where an employee spends a defined period in another team before deciding whether to transfer permanently.
- Certifications and self-directed study often accompany a move, particularly when the destination role calls for knowledge the person has not yet used on the job.

## What makes a transition succeed

- Success is less about titles and more about **demonstrated readiness**. Security disciplines share a common foundation, but each has its own tools, standards, and ways of thinking, so a credible move usually rests on evidence that the person has already started operating in the new domain.
- Hiring managers for internal roles weigh this track record alongside the candidate's reputation.
- A current manager's willingness to support a transfer, even at the short-term cost of losing a good contributor, is frequently the deciding factor, so approaching a move openly rather than quietly tends to keep relationships intact.

## How it relates to other growth

Internal mobility is specifically about **redirection**: applying existing strengths and trust to a different problem. It differs from continuous skill enhancement, which builds capability within a current role, and from leadership and management tracks, which expand responsibility rather than redirect it. Performance reviews often surface the aspirations that lead to a move, which is why candid conversations about where you want to go are a practical first step toward getting there.
