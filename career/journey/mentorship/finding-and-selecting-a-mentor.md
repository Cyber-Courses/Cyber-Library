# Finding and Selecting a Mentor

Choosing a mentor is the entry point to mentorship: the practitioner identifies someone whose experience can inform their own path and then opens a relationship. In a field as wide and fast-moving as cybersecurity, the **choice of mentor** shapes the quality of the guidance that follows, so it rewards deliberate thought rather than chance.

## Where good candidates come from

- Relationships already forming at work, in professional communities, and through conferences and local chapters.
- A manager a few levels up, a respected colleague on an adjacent team, an active member of a security community, or a speaker whose work resonates.
- Structured mentoring programs run by employers and professional bodies, which pair people deliberately and help those who lack an established network or want guidance outside their own employer.

## Weighing fit over seniority

- The most senior person is not always the best guide; relevant experience, willingness to invest time, and a learnable communication style matter more.
- Someone who has navigated the specific transition a mentee faces, such as moving from a generalist role into a specialization or from a technical track into leadership, often offers more useful perspective than a broader but less relevant expert.
- **Honesty, approachability, and genuine interest** in the mentee's growth distinguish a productive relationship from a nominal one.

## Making the first approach

- Lead with a modest, specific request rather than a sweeping ask for ongoing commitment.
- A concrete question about a decision, a field, or a piece of work invites a manageable response and lets both people gauge rapport before either commits to more.
- Being considerate of the mentor's time, prepared for conversations, and clear about what guidance is sought all make the relationship easier to sustain.

## How this stage connects

Selecting the right person first makes every later stage more productive. Once a mentor is found, the pair move on to [establishing goals and expectations](establishing-goals-and-expectations.md), may operate within a formal [program or a looser arrangement](mentorship-programs-and-structures.md), and ideally build a [relationship that lasts](long-term-mentoring-relationships.md) beyond any single question.
