# Outreach

**Outreach** is the practice of contacting people who can help with a job search before, or instead of, submitting a formal application. In a security career it means reaching out to recruiters, hiring managers, team members, and peers to learn about openings, express interest, and ask for guidance or a referral. Because many security roles are filled through personal connections rather than public postings, deliberate outreach often reaches opportunities that never appear on a job board.

## What effective outreach looks like

Good outreach is built on **relevance and brevity**.

- Name a specific role, team, or shared interest and explain concisely why you are a fit; generic mass messages rarely earn a reply.
- Reach out where security professionals gather: professional networks, conferences and local meetups, alumni groups, and communities organized around a technical specialty.
- Treat **warm introductions** from a mutual contact as the most valuable path, since they carry the most weight.

## Referrals

Referrals deserve particular attention because they matter a great deal in security hiring.

- An employee who vouches for a candidate helps an application clear the first screen and signals trust in a field where trust is central.
- Earning one usually follows from a genuine relationship or a demonstrated contribution, such as open source work, published research, or helpful participation in a community, rather than a cold request.
- Ask directly but respectfully, and make it easy for the referrer to say yes.

## Informational interviews

A lower-pressure form of outreach, distinct from directly asking for a job.

- The goal is to learn how a team works, what a role involves day to day, and what skills it rewards.
- These conversations build understanding, leave a positive impression, and sometimes lead to an opening later even when none exists now.
- They also help a candidate refine where their background fits, which sharpens every application that follows.

## Make it a habit

Treated as a steady professional habit rather than a burst of activity during unemployment, outreach compounds over a career. Relationships built while in one role frequently shape the next one. It connects closely to its neighbors without replacing them: [research](research.md) identifies who is worth contacting, [applications](applications.md) formalize interest once a door opens, [interviews](interviews.md) follow when a conversation turns into a process, and [negotiation](negotiation.md) comes last.
