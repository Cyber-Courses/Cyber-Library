# Security Firms

A security firm is a company whose **primary business is cybersecurity**, not a general technology company that happens to sell a security product on the side. The category covers **product vendors** in areas such as endpoint detection, identity, and network defense, and **service providers** that deliver penetration testing, managed detection and response, incident response, and threat intelligence. What unites them is that security is the whole enterprise rather than one division, which shapes their culture, hiring, and the depth of expertise they concentrate in a domain.

## Why they matter for a career

- They are among the most **specialized employers** in the field, with revenue that depends directly on security outcomes.
- They invest heavily in practitioner skill, maintain dedicated **research teams**, and expose staff to many client environments and adversary behavior.
- A junior analyst at a managed detection provider may see more real incidents in a year than a counterpart in a single in-house team sees in several.
- That intensity makes security firms a common place to **build credibility early**, and the names of well-regarded firms carry weight on a resume.

## What the work looks like

- **Service firms:** practitioners often work in a billable, engagement-driven model, rotating across customer environments, writing reports, and defending findings to clients.
- **Product vendors:** roles lean toward detection engineering, threat research, software development, and sales engineering that supports the product.
- Both settings reward deep technical skill, clear written and spoken communication, and the discipline to document work to a standard others can rely on.
- People typically enter through **analyst, researcher, or engineering** roles and grow by specializing, moving into lead or principal positions, or shifting toward client-facing and management tracks.

## How they differ from their neighbors

- **Technology firms** build security capability as part of a broader platform, so roles there blend security with general software or infrastructure work.
- **Consultancies** emphasize advisory relationships, governance, and program-level guidance rather than operating security services at scale.
- **Standards bodies** define the frameworks and expectations all of these organizations work against.

Many practitioners move among these settings over a career, and time at a focused security firm is frequently the foundation that makes those later moves possible.
