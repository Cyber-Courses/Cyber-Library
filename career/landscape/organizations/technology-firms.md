# Technology Firms

Technology firms build and operate software, hardware, cloud platforms, and consumer services rather than selling security as their primary product. They include large **cloud providers**, operating system and device makers, social and commerce platforms, and the many smaller software companies that ship products to customers. What sets them apart from security firms and consultancies is that **security is a supporting function** in service of a wider product mission, carried out inside the company that owns the systems being protected.

## Working at scale

- These firms are among the **largest employers** of security practitioners, and the work happens at significant scale.
- A professional at a major platform may help protect services used by millions of people, which raises both the stakes and the quality of the problems.
- **Compensation** at established firms is often strong, the engineering culture is mature, and practitioners gain exposure to modern infrastructure, automation, and software development practices.
- Many specializations, including **product security, cloud security, detection and response, and identity**, have deep representation inside these organizations.

## A day in in-house security

- Rather than running discrete client engagements, in-house teams **partner with developers** to review designs, assess code and architecture, build guardrails into continuous delivery pipelines, and respond to incidents affecting live services.
- The work is ongoing and relationship-driven: a practitioner is embedded in the organization's priorities, balances risk reduction against the pace of shipping, and measures success by how safely the product evolves over time.
- This contrasts with the time-boxed, externally scoped rhythm of a consultancy or a dedicated testing firm.

## Skills that translate

- The role calls for a blend of **security depth and software fluency**, since the environment is built and run by engineers and the ability to automate and to read or write code pays off.
- **Collaboration and influence** matter as much as technical skill: in-house teams rarely have direct authority over the product and must persuade engineering partners to adopt safer practices.
- Backgrounds in **software development, site reliability, systems administration, or security operations** all translate well, and many practitioners enter through an adjacent engineering role before specializing.

## Career paths

Careers tend to grow along two tracks, one toward **technical seniority** in a specialization such as application or cloud security, and one toward **leading teams and security programs**. Practitioners often move between technology firms and the neighboring organizations, bringing product experience to a security vendor or returning from consulting with a wider view of the field. Understanding where a technology firm sits relative to security firms, consultancies, and standards bodies helps you choose the setting that matches how you prefer to work.
