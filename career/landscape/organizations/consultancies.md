# Consultancies

Consultancies are professional services firms hired by other organizations to assess, design, and improve their security posture. Rather than defending a single enterprise or shipping a product, they sell **expertise and labor** across many clients through time-bound engagements. The category runs from the security and risk practices of large accounting and management firms, through midsize specialists, to small **boutiques** focused on one domain such as application security or governance.

## Why clients hire them

- **Independent judgment** that is not tied to any single product.
- **Capacity** they do not keep on staff, brought in for a defined piece of work.
- **Cross-industry experience** an internal team rarely accumulates on its own.

## What the work involves

- Work is organized around **engagements** with defined scopes, deliverables, and timelines.
- Typical mandates: assessing a program against a framework, designing a security architecture, running a penetration test, leading an incident response, or guiding a compliance effort toward a standard such as **SOC 2**, **ISO 27001**, or **PCI DSS**.
- Output is usually a report, a roadmap, a set of recommendations, or a remediated system, plus a briefing to the client's leadership.
- Much of a consultant's value lies in **translating findings into decisions** a business stakeholder can act on, so days mix technical analysis with client communication.
- **Utilization**, the share of time billed to clients, and clean movement from one engagement to the next shape how the work feels and how performance is measured.

## Breaking in and progressing

- Entrants often arrive from an internal security role, a technical specialty, or directly from a degree program into an **analyst** position at a larger firm, learning the trade on staffed engagements.
- **Certifications** carry weight because clients and firms use them as signals of competence.
- Broad exposure leads most consultants to develop a specialty over time, in offensive testing, cloud security, risk and compliance, or privacy.
- Progression moves from delivering work, to leading engagements and managing teams, to selling work and owning client relationships, with senior ranks blending technical credibility and business development.

## Where consultancies fit

Consultancies sit among the sibling organization types as **service providers rather than makers of technology**. Security firms and technology firms build and operate the products organizations buy, while standards bodies define the frameworks programs are measured against. Consultancies draw on all of these, applying vendor tools and recognized standards to a client's particular situation, and their independence from any single product is often part of what a client pays for.
