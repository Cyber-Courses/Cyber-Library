# IAM Analyst

An IAM analyst is the hands-on operator who keeps an organization's identity and access management program running day to day. The analyst administers user accounts, processes access requests, and confirms that people hold the right entitlements for the right reasons, working at the intersection of security, IT operations, and the business. Because identity has become the primary **control plane** for modern security, the quality of this routine work has direct consequences for an organization's exposure, and the role is one of the most common entry points into an identity career.

## Responsibilities

- Run the identity lifecycle, the **joiner, mover, and leaver** process: create accounts for new hires, adjust entitlements on role changes, and remove access promptly when people depart.
- Handle access requests through a ticketing or governance platform, confirming approvals are in place and grants align with **least privilege**.
- Coordinate periodic **access reviews and certifications**, gathering entitlement data and working with managers and application owners to confirm what each person should retain.
- Triage access-related issues and support authentication and single sign-on problems.
- Maintain group and role definitions and produce reports that demonstrate the program is working as intended.

## Skills and background

- **Technical foundations:** authentication versus authorization, role-based and attribute-based access control, directory services such as Active Directory and LDAP, and federation standards including SAML, OAuth, and OpenID Connect.
- **Tooling:** familiarity with an identity platform such as Okta, Microsoft Entra ID, SailPoint, or Ping, plus comfort in spreadsheets and reporting tools for analyzing large entitlement data sets.
- **Compliance awareness:** an understanding of drivers such as SOX, HIPAA, or ISO 27001 that explain why controls and evidence matter.
- **Soft skills:** attention to detail, careful documentation, and the communication to coordinate across security, help desk, and business teams.

## Getting into the role

People commonly arrive from a help desk, system administration, or general IT support background, and some enter directly from a degree or an entry-level security certification. Foundational credentials such as CompTIA Security+ or vendor-specific certifications from the identity platform in use are typical early milestones, and broader governance certifications from ISACA or (ISC)2 become relevant as responsibilities grow.

## Career progression

Deepening automation and platform skills leads toward the IAM engineer role, a focus on policy, certification, and audit leads toward an access governance specialist, and concentrating on high-risk credentials leads toward a PAM specialist. Analysts who develop a broad view of identity strategy may eventually move toward architecture. The lifecycle, review, and least-privilege practices the role teaches apply across every neighboring identity specialization, which makes it a durable foundation.

## Related roles

- [IAM Engineer](iam-engineer.md): builds the automation and integrations behind the processes the analyst runs by hand.
- [Access Governance Specialist](access-governance-specialist.md): owns the policy, certification, and audit side the analyst supports on the ground.
- [PAM Specialist](pam-specialist.md): concentrates on the high-risk administrative credentials the analyst otherwise handles only in general.
- [IAM Architect](iam-architect.md): sets the direction and standards the analyst operates within.
