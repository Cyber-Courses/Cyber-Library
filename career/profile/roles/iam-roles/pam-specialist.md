# PAM Specialist

A PAM specialist owns the controls that govern **privileged access**, the elevated rights that administrators, service accounts, and automated processes use to change systems rather than merely consume them. Because these accounts can reconfigure infrastructure, read sensitive data, and disable defenses, they are a primary target for attackers and a central concern for auditors. The specialist makes sure such access is vaulted, brokered, monitored, and granted only when it is genuinely needed, so that powerful credentials do not become standing liabilities.

## Responsibilities

- Onboard privileged accounts into a secure **vault** and configure automatic credential rotation.
- Set up **session brokering** so administrators connect through a controlled gateway rather than handling passwords directly.
- Build and refine policies for just-in-time elevation, approval workflows, and session recording.
- Discover unmanaged privileged accounts accumulated across servers, databases, network devices, and cloud environments.
- Support the people who depend on these controls, troubleshooting access issues without weakening the control when a stalled elevation request blocks production work.

## Skills and background

- **Systems knowledge:** authentication protocols, directory services, and operating system administration on both Windows and Linux.
- **Secrets fluency:** an understanding of how secrets flow through applications and pipelines.
- **Policy discipline:** the concepts that shape the program, **least privilege**, separation of duties, and the audit requirements privileged controls are built to satisfy.
- **Change management:** methodical testing and clear documentation, because a misconfigured rotation or broken session broker can lock administrators out of critical systems.

## Getting into the role

People commonly enter from system administration, identity and access management operations, or security operations, bringing firsthand experience of how privileged accounts are actually used. Many deepen their expertise through vendor platform training and broader identity security study before taking full ownership of a program.

## Career progression

With experience, a specialist may grow into senior or lead positions that set privileged access strategy, extend the practice into secrets management for applications and cloud workloads, or move toward broader identity architecture. The defining thread is a concentration on the highest-risk accounts, where the consequences of weak control are greatest.
