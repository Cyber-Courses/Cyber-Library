# IAM Architect

Designing how an organization manages digital identities, and the access those identities hold, is the work of the IAM architect. Where analysts and engineers operate and refine identity systems day to day, the architect defines the overall structure: the **authentication model**, the **authorization approach**, the directory and federation topology, and the standards that keep these elements coherent as the environment grows. The role sits where security architecture meets identity, and it depends less on hands-on administration than on the ability to reason about complex systems and translate business and security requirements into durable design.

## Responsibilities

- Produce reference architectures and target-state designs, and select and integrate identity platforms.
- Define patterns for single sign-on, federation, multifactor authentication, and lifecycle processes such as joiner, mover, and leaver handling.
- Review proposed integrations and set standards that application teams follow.
- Model how access should map to roles and attributes, weighing trade-offs between security, usability, and cost.
- Lead design reviews, write documentation, and advise engineering teams, since the value of the role lies in decisions many others build on for years.

## Skills and background

- **Protocol knowledge:** a solid grasp of authentication and authorization protocols such as SAML, OAuth 2.0, OpenID Connect, and Kerberos.
- **Platform breadth:** directory services, certificate and key concepts, and the identity features of major cloud platforms.
- **Architecture discipline:** understanding business processes, documenting decisions clearly, and communicating with both executives and engineers.
- **Accumulated depth:** broad experience gathered over years rather than a single specialty, since the role coordinates many moving parts.

## Getting into the role

Most architects do not start here. They typically arrive after years as an IAM engineer, a systems or security engineer, or an identity-focused consultant, and they often hold architecture or cloud certifications that signal breadth. The route is defined by accumulated operational experience as much as by any single credential.

## Career progression

Growth tends to follow scope, with architects taking responsibility for larger domains, enterprise-wide identity strategy, or specialized areas such as workload and machine identity. The architect's contribution is to ensure directories, federation, and access models fit together into a consistent whole, so progression often means owning more of that whole.

## Related roles

- [IAM Engineer](iam-engineer.md): builds and integrates the systems the architect specifies.
- [IAM Analyst](iam-analyst.md): handles access requests, reviews, and administration within those systems.
- [Access Governance Specialist](access-governance-specialist.md): concentrates on certification, policy, and compliance reporting.
- [PAM Specialist](pam-specialist.md): focuses specifically on securing privileged and administrative accounts.
