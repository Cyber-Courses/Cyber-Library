# Product Security Engineer

Security is a property of the product itself for a **product security engineer**, not a gate applied at the end. The role owns security across a company's products through their entire lifecycle, from early design through development, release, and ongoing operation. Embedded alongside product and engineering teams, the engineer reasons about how a feature could fail, what an attacker could do with it, and how the design can make whole classes of weakness unlikely or impossible. Treating the product as a shipping system, including its architecture, dependencies, build pipeline, and runtime, is what sets this role apart from narrower application-focused work.

## Responsibilities

- Review architecture and design proposals and perform **threat modeling** on new features.
- Partner with developers to resolve security findings before they reach customers.
- Define secure defaults and reusable patterns, such as authentication libraries, access control frameworks, and input handling guidance.
- Join design discussions and answer questions from engineers who want to build something correctly.
- Assess third-party components and support incident investigations that touch the product.
- Measure how well security practices are adopted across teams.

## Skills and background

- **Engineering foundation:** an understanding of how modern applications and cloud platforms are built, with the ability to read and write code in the languages the company uses.
- **Attacker's perspective:** a grasp of cryptography, identity, networking, and operating systems strong enough to reason about **trust boundaries**.
- **Systems thinking:** the ability to prioritize, since a product security program must focus effort where risk is greatest rather than chase every possible weakness.
- **Secure design knowledge:** familiarity with secure design principles, common vulnerability categories, and the practical realities of continuous delivery.
- **Influence:** communication skills, because the engineer rarely owns the code and must persuade teams to prioritize fixes.

## Getting into the role

People commonly enter product security from software engineering, from application security or penetration testing, or occasionally from site reliability and platform roles. An engineering background offers credibility with development teams and an instinct for what guidance is realistic to follow, while a testing background brings sharper intuition about how systems break.

## Career progression

Early on the work concentrates on reviews and threat models for individual features. With experience, engineers take on larger architectural decisions, shape the security strategy for a product area, and mentor others. Growth paths lead toward senior and principal engineering roles, toward security architecture, or into leadership of a product security function.

## Related roles

- [Application Security Engineer](application-security-engineer.md): concentrates on finding and remediating vulnerabilities in application code and services, where product security works at the level of the product's design and overall posture.
- [Secure Code Reviewer](secure-code-reviewer.md): focuses closely on the correctness of code itself.
- [DevSecOps Engineer](devsecops-engineer.md): centers on the pipeline and the automation that enforces security in delivery.
- [Security Champion](security-champion.md): a developer who carries security awareness within their own team.
