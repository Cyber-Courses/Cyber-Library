# Compliance Analyst

A compliance analyst makes sure an organization's security practices line up with the rules it is obligated to follow. Those obligations arrive from several directions at once: laws and regulations, industry standards such as **ISO 27001** or the **PCI DSS**, government frameworks such as the **NIST family**, and the security clauses written into customer contracts. The analyst translates these external requirements into concrete internal controls, then confirms that the controls exist, work as intended, and can be shown to work when someone asks.

## Responsibilities

- Map each requirement to the control that satisfies it, keeping the organization's control documentation current between reviews.
- Gather evidence that a control is operating, such as a configuration export, an access review, a policy document, or a completed training record.
- Prepare for audits and assessments: assemble evidence requests and walk auditors through how a process runs.
- Track audit findings through remediation until each one is closed.
- Monitor for control gaps and follow changes in regulations that affect the organization.

## Skills and background

- **Framework knowledge:** solid working command of common frameworks and how their control families relate to one another.
- **Technical literacy:** enough understanding to know what a control actually does and to read the evidence behind it.
- **Organizational skill:** the discipline to chase documentation across many teams without losing the thread.
- **Communication:** the ability to explain a requirement plainly and ask for evidence in terms the owning team understands.

## Getting into the role

People commonly enter from audit, IT operations, information security, or risk, and some arrive from adjacent fields such as accounting or quality assurance where documentation discipline is already second nature. Entry-level analysts often start by supporting a single framework or evidence-collection cycle, and foundational certifications plus hands-on exposure to one audit tend to build credibility quickly.

## Career progression

With experience, an analyst may specialize in a particular regime, take ownership of a full certification, or move toward broader governance work. As responsibilities grow, the natural progressions include ISACA's CISA for audit depth and the CISSP for broader security breadth.

## Related roles

- [Risk Analyst](risk-analyst.md): identifies and quantifies what could go wrong rather than proving adherence to a fixed rule set.
- [Policy Analyst](policy-analyst.md): drafts and maintains the internal rules that controls are meant to enforce.
- [Security Auditor](security-auditor.md): independently tests and attests to control effectiveness, usually from outside the team that operates the controls.
- [GRC Program Manager](grc-program-manager.md): coordinates these efforts across the enterprise and owns the overall program.
