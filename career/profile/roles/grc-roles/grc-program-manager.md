# GRC Program Manager

A GRC program manager owns the structure that holds an organization's governance, risk, and compliance work together. Rather than performing any single analysis, this role designs and runs the program that connects policy, risk treatment, and regulatory obligations into one coherent effort with clear ownership, schedules, and reporting. The position exists because security governance tends to fragment across teams and tools, and someone has to keep the pieces aligned, moving, and visible to leadership. It is a coordinating and accountable role more than a hands-on investigative one.

Day to day, the work is organized around keeping commitments on track. A program manager maintains the catalog of controls and the frameworks the organization follows, plans and sequences assessments and audits so they do not collide, and tracks remediation items through to closure. They translate risk findings and compliance gaps into prioritized plans, manage the calendar of recurring obligations such as attestations and reviews, and prepare the summaries that let executives and boards understand where the program stands. Much of the value lies in reporting that is accurate and timely, since leaders act on the picture the program manager presents.

The role calls for breadth across the GRC disciplines rather than deep specialization in one. A program manager needs a working understanding of common frameworks and regulations, familiarity with how risk is assessed and controls are evaluated, and the project management skill to drive work across teams that do not report to them. Strong writing, calm facilitation, and the judgment to prioritize competing demands matter as much as technical knowledge. Because the role depends on influence rather than authority, the ability to build trust with engineering, legal, audit, and business stakeholders is central to being effective.

People commonly enter this role after time as a risk analyst, compliance analyst, or auditor, where they learn the substance of the work before taking responsibility for coordinating it. Others arrive from project or program management backgrounds and build their domain knowledge on the job, often supported by certifications in security management or audit. With experience, a program manager may grow into leadership of the wider GRC function, into roles such as head of risk or compliance, or toward the chief information security officer track. The position neighbors the risk analyst and compliance analyst, who produce the assessments and evidence it schedules and reports on, the policy analyst, who drafts the governance documents it operationalizes, and the security auditor, whose reviews it coordinates and whose findings it drives to resolution.
