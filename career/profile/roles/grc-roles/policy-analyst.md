# Policy Analyst

The documented backbone of a security program, the statements that tell employees, administrators, and vendors what is required, permitted, and prohibited, comes from the policy analyst. Working within the governance, risk, and compliance function, the analyst translates external drivers such as laws, regulations, contracts, and recognized frameworks, together with internal business goals, into **security policies, standards, and guidelines**. Clear, current policy matters because it is the reference point auditors test against, the authority that enables enforcement, and the mechanism that turns abstract risk decisions into expectations people can follow.

## Responsibilities

- Draft new policy documents, revise existing ones as technology and regulations change, and retire those that no longer apply.
- Map controls to frameworks such as the **NIST Cybersecurity Framework**, **ISO/IEC 27001**, or the **CIS Controls**.
- Reconcile overlapping requirements so a single internal standard can satisfy several external obligations at once.
- Gather input from legal, human resources, IT operations, and engineering, then run documents through review and approval cycles.
- Field interpretation questions and grant or document exceptions when a strict reading does not fit a situation.

## Skills and background

- **Writing and structure:** the ability to express precise requirements in plain language and maintain a coherent document hierarchy.
- **Careful reasoning:** attention to edge cases and unintended consequences before a policy reaches the people who must implement it.
- **Domain knowledge:** a working command of control frameworks, regulatory regimes such as GDPR, HIPAA, or PCI DSS, and general security concepts.
- **Collaboration:** diplomacy for resolving conflicts between what is ideal, what is feasible, and what the business will accept.

## Getting into the role

Backgrounds vary widely. Some analysts come from compliance, audit, legal, or operations roles, while others move over from technical security positions and lean on their hands-on experience. People typically enter from an adjacent GRC or administrative position. Certifications such as ISACA's CISM or CRISC, or (ISC)2's CISSP, can signal the governance knowledge the role calls for.

## Career progression

An analyst grows by taking ownership of a broader policy portfolio, then moving into policy program leadership, a GRC program manager track, or a specialized governance or privacy role.

## Related roles

- [Security Auditor](security-auditor.md): independently evaluates whether controls and documents hold up under scrutiny.
