# Forensics and IR Roles

Forensics and incident response roles answer the question of what happened when a system is compromised or when electronic evidence must be preserved, then help the organization recover, learn, and meet its obligations. The work is investigative rather than adversarial: it reconstructs events from the traces that systems and applications leave behind. Because the conclusions often inform operational decisions, legal proceedings, and regulatory reporting, these professions prize accuracy, careful evidence handling, and clear documentation above all.

## What these roles share

- **Evidence discipline:** verified acquisition, sound chain of custody, and reproducible methods that another examiner could follow.
- **Defensible conclusions:** findings stated to withstand scrutiny from a courtroom, a regulator, an insurer, or an internal review.
- **Clear writing:** reports and briefings that non-technical stakeholders can act on.
- **Different tempos:** some roles respond in real time to active threats, while others work methodically through preserved data long after an event.

## Roles

- [Digital Forensics Analyst](digital-forensics-analyst.md): recovers and interprets artifacts from devices and media to build a defensible timeline of events.
- [Incident Responder](incident-responder.md): coordinates the reaction to active or suspected compromise, containing the threat and directing recovery.
- [Malware Reverse Engineer](malware-reverse-engineer.md): analyzes malicious code to determine its behavior, capabilities, and indicators.
- [eDiscovery Specialist](ediscovery-specialist.md): manages the identification, preservation, and production of electronically stored information for legal matters.
- [Forensics Manager](forensics-manager.md): leads the function, setting standards and allocating resources so investigations stay consistent and defensible.
