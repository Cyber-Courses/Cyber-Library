# Forensics and IR Roles

Forensics and incident response roles share a common purpose: to understand what happened when a system is compromised or when electronic evidence must be preserved, and to help an organization recover, learn, and meet its obligations. These professions value accuracy, careful handling of evidence, and clear documentation, because their conclusions often inform operational decisions, legal proceedings, and regulatory reporting. The work is investigative rather than adversarial, focused on reconstructing events from the traces that systems and applications leave behind.

Although the roles are related, they differ in scope, pace, and the questions they answer. Some respond in real time to active threats, while others work methodically through preserved data long after an event. The pages beneath this section describe each role in turn, including its typical responsibilities, the skills it draws on, and how it fits alongside security operations, legal, and governance functions.

The [Digital Forensics Analyst](digital-forensics-analyst.md) examines computers, mobile devices, and storage media to recover and interpret artifacts, establishing a defensible timeline of events while maintaining a sound chain of custody. The [Incident Responder](incident-responder.md) coordinates the organization's reaction to active or suspected compromise, containing the threat, directing recovery, and capturing lessons that strengthen future defense. The [Malware Reverse Engineer](malware-reverse-engineer.md) analyzes malicious code to determine its behavior, capabilities, and indicators, producing the technical understanding that both responders and analysts rely on.

Two further roles connect this work to legal and organizational needs. The [eDiscovery Specialist](ediscovery-specialist.md) manages the identification, preservation, and production of electronically stored information for litigation, investigations, and regulatory requests, applying defined legal standards to large volumes of data. The [Forensics Manager](forensics-manager.md) leads these functions, setting standards and procedures, allocating resources, and ensuring that investigations are consistent, documented, and aligned with the organization's obligations.

Careers in this area rarely follow a single path. Many practitioners begin in operations or analysis and move toward investigation, specialized reverse engineering, or leadership over time, and the boundaries between these roles vary from one organization to another. Reading the individual pages alongside this overview helps a professional see where their interests fit and how the roles complement one another and the wider security program.
