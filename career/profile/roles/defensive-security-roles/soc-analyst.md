# SOC Analyst

A SOC analyst works inside a **security operations center**, the team that continuously watches an organization's networks, systems, and accounts for signs of malicious activity. The analyst is the frontline of defensive work: the person who sees an alert first, decides whether it reflects a real problem, and sets the response in motion. Because it is where raw telemetry becomes a judgment that something is wrong, the role is one of the most common and accessible entry points into security, and the experience it builds underpins many later specializations.

## Responsibilities

- Monitor and work a queue of alerts from a **SIEM**, endpoint detection platforms, and network sensors.
- Triage each alert: gather context from logs and related events, then classify it as a false positive, a low-risk anomaly, or a genuine threat.
- Escalate confirmed incidents to response teams with the detail they need to act.
- Document reasoning and actions clearly so an investigation can be picked up by anyone.
- Feed detection tuning: flag noisy rules and suggest refinements so future alerts are more accurate.

## Skills and background

- **Foundations:** how operating systems, networks, authentication, and logging actually behave, so abnormal activity stands out against normal.
- **Attacker awareness:** the shapes common attacks take and the artifacts they leave; a framework such as MITRE ATT&CK helps describe findings consistently.
- **Tooling:** comfort with SIEM, endpoint, and ticketing platforms, and basic query skills to pull and correlate events.
- **Temperament:** steady attention during quiet periods, disciplined note-taking, curiosity about why an alert fired, and composure when activity spikes.

## Getting into the role

Most analysts arrive from help desk, IT support, system administration, or networking, bringing a practical sense of how systems behave in production. Others enter through a security degree, structured training, or a foundational certification such as **CompTIA Security+** or **CySA+** that signals baseline knowledge. It is a realistic first security job, and many centers run tiered shifts in which junior analysts handle initial triage while senior analysts take the harder cases.

## Career progression

Inside a SOC, analysts typically move from triage toward investigation, detection engineering, or shift leadership. The breadth of real activity the role exposes makes it a strong base for branching into incident response, threat hunting, forensics, or threat intelligence as interests sharpen.

## Related roles
