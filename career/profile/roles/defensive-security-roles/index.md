# Defensive Security Roles

Defensive security roles share a common purpose: to detect malicious activity, understand what it means, and limit the harm it can cause. Often described collectively as blue team work, these professions keep watch over an organization's systems and data, turning raw signals into informed decisions. Their value lies in sustained vigilance and disciplined analysis, so that threats are caught early and handled before they escalate.

Although the roles are related, they differ in tempo, depth, and the questions they ask. Some concentrate on continuous monitoring and first assessment, others on deep investigation of a confirmed event, and still others on the careful study of hostile code or the deliberate search for threats that automated tooling has missed. The pages beneath this section describe each role in turn, including its typical responsibilities, the skills it draws on, and how it fits alongside offensive and governance work.

The SOC Analyst works at the front line of the security operations center, monitoring alerts, triaging suspicious activity, and escalating what warrants a closer look. The Incident Responder takes over when an event is confirmed, coordinating the investigation, containment, and recovery that restore normal operation and capture lessons for the future. Both roles depend on clear communication and sound judgment under time pressure.

Two further roles reach into specialized analysis. The Malware Analyst examines hostile software to understand its behavior, capabilities, and indicators, producing knowledge that sharpens detection and guides response. The Threat Hunter proactively searches an environment for signs of compromise that existing controls have not flagged, forming hypotheses from current intelligence and testing them against available data.

Careers in this area rarely follow a single path. Many practitioners begin in the security operations center and grow into investigation, analysis, or hunting as their interests develop, and the boundaries between these roles vary from one organization to another. Reading the individual pages alongside this overview helps a professional see where their strengths fit and how the roles reinforce one another and the wider security program.
