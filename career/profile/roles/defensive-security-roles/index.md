# Defensive Security Roles

Defensive security roles, often grouped together as **blue team** work, share one purpose: detect malicious activity, understand what it means, and limit the harm it can cause. These professions keep watch over an organization's systems and data, turning raw telemetry into informed decisions so that threats are caught early and handled before they escalate. What sets them apart from one another is tempo and depth, from continuous monitoring to deep investigation of hostile code to the deliberate pursuit of threats no tool has flagged.

## What these roles share

- **Sustained vigilance:** value comes from disciplined attention over time, not one-off heroics.
- **Evidence-driven judgment:** decisions rest on logs, telemetry, and artifacts rather than hunches.
- **Clear communication:** findings are only useful when responders and leadership can act on them.
- **A common language of attacker behavior:** frameworks such as MITRE ATT&CK let the roles describe and hand off findings consistently.

## Roles

- [SOC Analyst](soc-analyst.md): monitors alerts at the front line of the security operations center, triages suspicious activity, and escalates what warrants a closer look.
- [Malware Analyst](malware-analyst.md): dissects hostile software to understand its behavior, capabilities, and indicators, producing knowledge that sharpens detection and guides response.
- [Threat Hunter](threat-hunter.md): proactively searches an environment for compromise that existing controls have missed, forming hypotheses from current intelligence and testing them against data.

Careers here rarely follow a single path. Many practitioners begin in the security operations center and grow into analysis or hunting as their interests develop, and confirmed incidents pass to the [Incident Responder](../forensics-and-ir-roles/incident-responder.md), who coordinates containment and recovery. The boundaries between all of these roles vary from one organization to another.
