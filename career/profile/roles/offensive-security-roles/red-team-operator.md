# Red Team Operator

Where a penetration test catalogs weaknesses, a red team operator pursues a single objective the way a real attacker would. The operator runs **goal-based, full-scope campaigns** that emulate a credible threat actor, working toward a defined outcome such as reaching a specific dataset or demonstrating a particular business impact, and does so in a way that tests people, process, and technology together. The defining question is not only whether a path into the organization exists, but whether the organization notices and responds when that path is taken.

## Responsibilities

- Plan and run engagements that unfold over weeks rather than hours.
- Study the target environment and select techniques that mirror a chosen threat actor.
- Operate beneath the defenders' detection thresholds while making progress toward the objective.
- Manage **command and control**, maintaining a quiet, resilient channel to compromised systems.
- Weigh each action against the engagement goals and the risk of being caught.
- Keep careful records of what was done and when, so the campaign can be reconstructed and compared against what defenders observed.

## Skills and background

- **Technical breadth:** a broad foundation across operating systems, networks, identity systems, and common enterprise software.
- **Defensive awareness:** a strong grasp of how defensive tooling and security operations teams work, so activity can be shaped around them.
- **Judgment:** patience and discipline to balance progress against stealth and the rules of engagement.
- **Communication:** clear writing that turns a campaign into lessons defenders and leadership can act on.

## Getting into the role

Most operators arrive after experience in a related discipline rather than starting here directly. A common path runs through **penetration testing** or **security operations**, where a practitioner builds the technical range and operational awareness that red teaming demands. Recognized training and certifications in adversary emulation can support the move, though demonstrated experience carries particular weight.

## Career progression

Growth tends to move from executing portions of a campaign under guidance, toward leading engagements, designing scenarios tied to specific threats, and eventually shaping a team's methodology and its relationship with the organizations it serves. From there, operators often influence detection strategy and broader security architecture.
