# Penetration Tester

A penetration tester runs authorized, scoped assessments that find and demonstrate weaknesses before a genuine adversary can reach them. The work spans **web applications**, **networks**, and **infrastructure**, always inside clear **rules of engagement** that fix what may be tested, when, and to what depth. For many practitioners it is the entry point into offensive security, where technical investigation meets the discipline of working safely within agreed boundaries.

## Responsibilities

- Plan each engagement against a defined scope and goal, confirming what is in and out of bounds.
- Examine the target to understand how it is built and where it may be weak.
- Verify which weaknesses are real rather than theoretical, and judge their practical impact.
- Rate the severity of each finding and prioritize what matters most within a limited window.
- Write the report: explain findings in plain terms and give remediation guidance developers and administrators can act on.
- Protect client systems and data throughout, keeping meticulous notes of what was done.

## Skills and background

- **Technical foundations:** a working understanding of how applications, operating systems, and networks fit together.
- **Methodology:** familiarity with recognized testing frameworks and the judgment to prioritize within a time box, rather than a single narrow specialty.
- **Tooling:** comfort with assessment platforms and the ability to validate findings by hand instead of trusting scanner output.
- **Communication:** clear writing and calm client interaction that reach both technical staff and business stakeholders.
- **Professional discipline:** careful scoping, respect for client environments, and honest reporting of impact.

## Getting into the role

People commonly arrive from **system administration**, **software development**, **network engineering**, or **security operations**, bringing a feel for how real systems behave. Many build credibility through hands-on practice, structured study, and well-regarded certifications before taking on client work, such as the training offered through [Offensive Security, Penetration Testing with Kali Linux](https://www.offsec.com/) and the [SANS Institute, Penetration Testing and Ethical Hacking Resources](https://www.sans.org/).

## Career progression

With experience, a tester can grow into senior or lead roles that own scoping and client relationships, or specialize in areas such as web application or cloud assessment. The role also opens toward broader offensive disciplines like red teaming, and it strengthens vulnerability management and security engineering by feeding verified findings back into the defensive program.

## Related roles

- [Red Team Operator](red-team-operator.md): runs broader, objective-based campaigns that test detection and response over longer periods, where a pen test aims for breadth of findings.
- [Purple Team Specialist](purple-team-specialist.md): turns offensive findings into detection improvements through collaboration rather than scoped reporting.
- [Vulnerability Researcher](vulnerability-researcher.md): discovers unknown flaws without a fixed scope, where a tester applies known weaknesses against a defined target.
