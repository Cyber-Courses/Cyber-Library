# Cloud Security Analyst

Cloud accounts generate a continuous stream of signals that need informed interpretation before they are useful to a security program, and a **cloud security analyst** is the person who provides it. The work centers on assessing configuration, access, and activity across platforms such as Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud, then translating what the platform reports into a clear picture of **risk**. It is more observational and analytical than hands-on with building infrastructure, and its value lies in sustained visibility.

## Responsibilities

- Review the **security posture** of cloud accounts and the resources inside them, looking for misconfigurations, overly broad permissions, exposed storage, unencrypted data, and gaps against internal baselines.
- Monitor logs and alerts from native services and from cloud security posture management and workload protection tools.
- Triage alerts, separating genuine concerns from noise.
- Investigate the context behind a concern, document the finding, and rate its severity.
- Route findings to the team that can fix them, with a write-up that explains what is wrong, why it matters, and what a reasonable remediation looks like.

## Skills and background

- **Platform knowledge:** a solid grasp of how cloud platforms are structured, including identity and access management, networking, storage, and logging, plus shared responsibility and common control frameworks.
- **Investigation:** comfort reading logs and querying data, with familiarity across at least one major provider.
- **Attacker awareness:** a working knowledge of how attacks against cloud resources tend to unfold, so meaningful patterns stand out.
- **Trust-building habits:** careful verification, consistent documentation, sound prioritization, and communication that reaches both technical staff and the people accountable for a service.

## Getting into the role

People commonly enter from security operations, help desk or system administration, network roles, or general IT, often after adding cloud fundamentals and a provider certification to an existing security foundation. Early work tends to emphasize monitoring and triage.

## Career progression

With experience an analyst takes on more complex assessments, tuning of detection logic, and ownership of posture across larger estates. Some deepen their defensive focus toward cloud security operations or detection engineering, while others shift toward building and hardening systems or toward governance work.
