# Cloud Compliance Specialist

Where engineering roles build and operate cloud defenses, a **cloud compliance specialist** is concerned with whether those defenses satisfy a defined set of obligations and whether that satisfaction can be demonstrated to auditors, customers, and regulators. The role translates abstract requirements, such as those in **SOC 2**, **ISO 27001**, **PCI DSS**, **HIPAA**, **FedRAMP**, or **GDPR**, into concrete expectations for how cloud accounts, services, and data are configured and governed. It serves as a bridge between the technical organization and the people who need assurance.

## Responsibilities

- Maintain the crosswalk between external frameworks and the organization's internal control set.
- Gather and review evidence such as configuration exports and access records, confirming that controls operate as intended.
- Track gaps through to remediation with the teams that own them.
- Prepare for and support audits and customer security reviews.
- Draft policies and standards, and assess the compliance posture of cloud providers and third-party services.
- Lean on compliance-as-code and continuous monitoring, so control status is measured against live cloud configuration rather than reconstructed by hand at audit time.

## Skills and background

- **Platform grasp:** a solid understanding of how major cloud platforms are structured, including identity and access management, logging, encryption, and network boundaries.
- **Framework fluency:** command of the relevant frameworks and the ability to read a regulation and reason about what it demands in practice.
- **Communication:** strong writing, organization, and stakeholder skills, since the specialist explains requirements to engineers and technical reality to auditors and executives.
- **Interpretive judgment:** attention to detail and comfort with ambiguity, because requirements written for an earlier era of on-premises systems must be interpreted sensibly for the cloud.

## Getting into the role

People commonly enter from governance, risk, and compliance backgrounds, from audit or consulting, or from a technical cloud or security position where they developed an interest in the regulatory side. Credentials such as **CISA**, **CCSK**, the cloud providers' own certifications, and broader security certifications are common signals, though demonstrated judgment and a record of successful audits carry more weight over time.

## Career progression

Growth tends to move toward leading an audit program, specializing in a demanding regime such as FedRAMP, or stepping into wider risk management and security governance leadership.
