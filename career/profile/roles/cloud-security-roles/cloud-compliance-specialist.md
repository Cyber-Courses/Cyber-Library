# Cloud Compliance Specialist

A Cloud Compliance Specialist ensures that an organization's use of cloud services aligns with the laws, regulations, contractual commitments, and control frameworks that apply to it. Where engineering roles build and operate cloud defenses, the compliance specialist is concerned with whether those defenses satisfy a defined set of obligations and whether that satisfaction can be demonstrated to auditors, customers, and regulators. The role translates abstract requirements, such as those found in SOC 2, ISO 27001, PCI DSS, HIPAA, FedRAMP, or GDPR, into concrete expectations for how cloud accounts, services, and data are configured and governed.

The day-to-day work centers on mapping requirements to controls and then confirming that those controls operate as intended. A specialist maintains the crosswalk between external frameworks and the organization's internal control set, gathers and reviews evidence such as configuration exports and access records, and tracks gaps through to remediation with the teams that own them. Much of the role involves preparing for and supporting audits and customer security reviews, drafting policies and standards, and assessing the compliance posture of cloud providers and third-party services. Increasingly the work leans on compliance-as-code and continuous monitoring, so that control status is measured against live cloud configuration rather than reconstructed by hand at audit time.

The role calls for a solid grasp of how major cloud platforms are structured, including identity and access management, logging, encryption, and network boundaries, paired with fluency in the relevant frameworks and the ability to read a regulation and reason about what it demands in practice. Strong writing, organization, and stakeholder skills matter a great deal, because a specialist spends considerable time explaining requirements to engineers and explaining technical reality to auditors and executives. Attention to detail and comfort with ambiguity are both important, since requirements are often written for an earlier era of on-premises systems and must be interpreted sensibly for the cloud.

People commonly enter the role from governance, risk, and compliance backgrounds, from audit or consulting, or from a technical cloud or security position where they developed an interest in the regulatory side. Credentials such as CISA, CCSK, the cloud providers' own certifications, and broader security certifications are common signals, though demonstrated judgment and a record of successful audits carry more weight over time. Growth tends to move toward leading an audit program, specializing in a demanding regime such as FedRAMP, or stepping into wider risk management and security governance leadership.

The specialist works alongside the other cloud security roles without duplicating them. Cloud Security Engineers and DevSecOps Engineers build and automate the controls the specialist relies on as evidence, Cloud Security Analysts monitor the environment for events that compliance frameworks require organizations to detect and handle, and Cloud Security Architects design the structures within which compliant operation becomes feasible. The compliance specialist connects these efforts to the external obligations that justify them, and so serves as a bridge between the technical organization and the auditors, regulators, and customers who need assurance.
