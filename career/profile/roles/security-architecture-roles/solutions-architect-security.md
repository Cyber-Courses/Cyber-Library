# Solutions Architect - Security

A solutions architect for security designs the security of **one specific solution**, a defined product, application, platform, or business initiative, rather than the organization's architecture as a whole. Where an enterprise or domain architect works at the level of broad standards and reference models, this role operates close to a **single delivery effort** and translates those standards into a concrete, buildable design. It pairs an understanding of how systems are actually constructed with the discipline of thinking through threats, controls, and trade-offs before code and infrastructure are committed.

## Responsibilities

- Gather security and compliance requirements for a given project.
- Produce design artifacts such as architecture diagrams and control mappings.
- Perform **threat modeling** to identify where a proposed design could be abused or fail.
- Specify how identity, access management, encryption, logging, network segmentation, and data protection are realized in that particular context.
- Weigh those choices against cost, performance, and delivery timelines.
- Explain security decisions to product managers, developers, and operations teams and negotiate outcomes that are secure enough without stalling the initiative.

## Skills and background

- **Technical depth:** a working knowledge of cloud platforms, networking, application architecture, and modern delivery practices such as containers and continuous integration.
- **Adversarial reasoning:** the ability to read and reason about how a system behaves under both normal and adversarial conditions.
- **Design discipline:** the analytical habits of risk assessment and familiarity with common control frameworks and secure design patterns that map abstract requirements onto specific technologies.
- **Communication:** the skill to document a design clearly and defend it in review.
- **Credentials:** background often includes study aligned with cloud security and architecture, though demonstrated design experience usually matters more than any single certification.

## Getting into the role

People most often enter after several years as a security engineer, a software or cloud engineer, or a security analyst who has gravitated toward design questions. The natural progression is from implementing controls to specifying them, and then to owning the security design of larger and more ambiguous solutions.

## Career progression

With experience, a solutions architect may move toward broader architecture roles that span many systems, deepen into a particular technical domain, or take on leadership of a security engineering function. The position rewards curiosity about how things are built and a willingness to keep learning as platforms and threats evolve.
