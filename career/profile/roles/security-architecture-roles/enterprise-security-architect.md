# Enterprise Security Architect

Where most architects focus on a product, platform, or domain, the enterprise security architect takes the **widest view**. This senior practitioner defines how security is structured across an entire organization, setting the target state as principles, reference architectures, and standards, and making sure the controls chosen in individual programs add up to a **coherent, defensible whole**. The role connects business strategy, risk appetite, and regulatory obligations to the technical and organizational decisions that carry them out.

## Responsibilities

- Develop and maintain the organization's security reference architecture.
- Review major initiatives to confirm they fit the intended model, and resolve tension between competing designs so controls do not become inconsistent or redundant.
- Contribute to roadmaps that retire legacy weaknesses and fund new capabilities.
- Set standards that domain and solution architects apply in their own areas.
- Advise leadership on the security implications of large decisions such as a cloud migration, an acquisition, or a new line of business.
- Translate business risk into clear architectural direction and explain technical trade-offs to executives in terms they can weigh.

## Skills and background

- **Breadth over depth:** a solid grasp of identity, network, application, data, and cloud security, valued more than deep specialization in any one tool.
- **Governance fluency:** familiarity with risk management and at least one recognized architecture framework such as SABSA, TOGAF, or the Open Group guidance many programs adopt.
- **Soft skills:** judgment, written communication, and the ability to influence without direct authority, since the architect guides many teams rather than owning the systems.
- **Temperament:** comfort with ambiguity and long time horizons, because architectural decisions shape the organization for years.

## Getting into the role

People typically reach this role after substantial experience rather than entering it directly. Common paths run through security engineering, solutions architecture, or a domain-focused architecture role, often reinforced by credentials such as CISSP, CISSP-ISSAP, or the SABSA certifications, together with a track record of delivering secure systems at scale.

## Career progression

From here an architect may grow toward a chief architect position, a head of security architecture, or a broader leadership role such as CISO, and some deepen instead into specialized advisory work. The role sits above and alongside its neighbors, relying on engineers and solution architects to realize its direction and on domain architects to carry its standards into specialized areas.
