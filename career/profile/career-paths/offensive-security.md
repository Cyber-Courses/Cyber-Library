# Offensive Security

Built around **authorized adversary simulation**, offensive security has practitioners adopt an attacker's mindset to find weaknesses in systems, applications, and organizations, then demonstrate those weaknesses safely so defenders can fix them. The track produces concrete, verified evidence of risk rather than theoretical concern, and that evidence is often what moves an organization to invest in remediation. Working offensively also builds a deep, practical understanding of how systems fail, which is valuable well beyond the track itself.

## Roles on this path

- [Penetration Tester](../roles/offensive-security-roles/penetration-tester.md): performs scoped, time-boxed assessments and reports verified weaknesses with clear remediation guidance.
- [Red Team Operator](../roles/offensive-security-roles/red-team-operator.md): runs full-scope, stealth-focused campaigns that test an organization's detection and response.
- [Purple Team Specialist](../roles/offensive-security-roles/purple-team-specialist.md): runs collaborative adversary emulation with defenders to validate and improve detection and response coverage.
- [Vulnerability Researcher](../roles/offensive-security-roles/vulnerability-researcher.md): discovers previously unknown vulnerabilities through source and binary analysis, paired with responsible disclosure.
- [Exploit Developer](../roles/offensive-security-roles/exploit-developer.md): turns vulnerabilities into dependable proof-of-concept code through deep knowledge of low-level internals and mitigations.

## Skills and certifications

- **Breadth of systems:** a working understanding of how networks, operating systems, applications, and identity systems fit together.
- **Methodology:** familiarity with recognized testing methodologies and the patience to investigate carefully within a limited window.
- **Rules of engagement:** careful scoping, written authorization, and the professional discipline to stay inside agreed boundaries.
- **Communication:** meticulous note-taking and reporting that reaches both engineers and business stakeholders, since the deliverable is a clear account of what was found and what to change.
- **Credentials:** well-regarded certifications and structured study, such as resources from [Offensive Security](https://www.offsec.com/) and [SANS](https://www.sans.org/), build credibility before client or internal work.

## How to progress

People commonly enter from system administration, software development, network engineering, or security operations, bringing an understanding of how real systems behave under pressure. Progression typically starts with scoped assessment work and broadens toward objective-driven campaigns, specialized research, and eventually lead roles that own engagement strategy and client relationships, and practitioners often move among the roles above as their interests sharpen. The track exists to strengthen defenses, so its findings feed directly into defensive security operations, application security, cloud security, and security architecture; it draws on threat intelligence to model realistic adversaries and informs the priorities that governance and risk programs weigh.
