# IAM

**Identity and Access Management**, usually shortened to **IAM**, establishes who and what can access an organization's systems and ensures each identity has only the access it genuinely needs. It covers the full life cycle of an identity, from the moment an account is created, through every change in role, to the point it is removed, and it spans the people, services, and devices that interact with modern environments. As organizations have moved toward cloud platforms, remote work, and interconnected applications, identity has become a primary security boundary, placing IAM at the center of how access is granted, proven, and revoked.

## Roles on this path

- [IAM Analyst](../roles/iam-roles/iam-analyst.md): administers identities, processes access requests, and supports the daily operation of identity and access management programs.
- [IAM Engineer](../roles/iam-roles/iam-engineer.md): designs, implements, and maintains the identity and access infrastructure that controls who can reach an organization's systems and data.
- [IAM Architect](../roles/iam-roles/iam-architect.md): designs the systems, standards, and controls governing how people and machines authenticate and gain access across an organization.
- [Access Governance Specialist](../roles/iam-roles/access-governance-specialist.md): runs the reviews, certifications, and policies that keep entitlements aligned with business need.
- [PAM Specialist](../roles/iam-roles/pam-specialist.md): designs and operates the controls governing privileged accounts, credentials, and sessions across an organization.

## Skills and certifications

- **Core concepts:** a solid grasp of authentication and authorization and the principle of **least privilege**, pursued through access reviews and certification campaigns.
- **Protocols and standards:** familiarity with **SAML**, **OAuth**, **OpenID Connect**, **LDAP**, and **SCIM**; the [NIST Digital Identity Guidelines (SP 800-63-4)](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/) frame identity assurance.
- **Platforms:** working knowledge of directory and identity platforms across on-premises and cloud settings.
- **Business fluency:** translating access policy into practical role designs and collaborating with application owners and auditors, since IAM touches nearly every department.
- **Automation:** scripting that brings consistency to repetitive identity processes, including the growing remit of privileged access and non-human identities; [ISACA](https://www.isaca.org/) publishes relevant IAM resources.

## How to progress

People commonly enter from system administration, help desk and directory support, cloud administration, or a general security operations background, bringing an understanding of how accounts and permissions behave in real environments. Early work tends to be operational, handling access requests and account life cycle tasks, before growing into engineering roles that build integrations and automate provisioning, and then into governance and architecture roles that define access models across the organization. Senior paths lead toward identity architecture, program ownership, or leadership of an identity function. IAM sits close to security architecture, which sets the broader design it implements, and to GRC, which relies on IAM evidence for access certifications; it also supports cloud security, where identity is often the main control plane.
