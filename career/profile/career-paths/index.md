# Career Paths

A **career path** is the long-term direction a professional takes through the field. Cybersecurity offers many paths rather than a single ladder, so understanding the common shapes of progression helps a person plan deliberately instead of drifting from role to role. Most people build broad foundations early, then branch: some deepen into a single domain and become recognized specialists, while others broaden across domains toward architecture or program ownership. A recurring choice is the **technical versus leadership** question, where technical tracks grow depth and influence close to hands-on work and leadership tracks move toward managing people, programs, and strategy.

## Paths

- [Offensive Security](offensive-security.md): authorized adversary simulation that finds and safely demonstrates weaknesses before real attackers do.
- [Defensive Security](defensive-security.md): blue team work that monitors, detects, and responds to threats and hardens systems against attack.
- [DFIR](dfir.md): digital forensics and incident response, investigating incidents and guiding an organization back to a trusted state.
- [Threat Intelligence](threat-intelligence.md): collecting and analyzing adversary knowledge so defenses can anticipate and prioritize.
- [Application Security](application-security.md): building security into software across design, development, and deployment.
- [Cloud Security](cloud-security.md): protecting configuration, identity, data, and workloads on public and hybrid cloud platforms.
- [IAM](iam.md): governing digital identities and controlling access across systems, applications, and data.
- [Security Architecture](security-architecture.md): designing systems and controls so security is built in rather than bolted on.
- [GRC](grc.md): governance, risk, and compliance that give a security program structure and accountability.
- [Privacy and Data Protection](privacy-and-data-protection.md): safeguarding personal data across its lifecycle through governance, engineering, and regulatory practice.
