# Defensive Security

Often called **blue team** work, defensive security is the career path devoted to protecting an organization's systems, data, and people from attack. Where offensive specialists probe for weaknesses, defenders build and operate the controls, monitoring, and response capabilities that keep an environment resilient under real conditions. The path is broad, spanning security operations, detection engineering, incident response, and the day-to-day hardening of networks and endpoints, and it is unified by one goal: reducing the likelihood and impact of a successful compromise. Because every organization that depends on technology needs this capability, it is one of the largest and most durable areas of the field.

## Roles on this path

- [SOC Analyst](../roles/defensive-security-roles/soc-analyst.md): monitors the environment, triages alerts, and escalates genuine threats for deeper response.
- [Threat Hunter](../roles/defensive-security-roles/threat-hunter.md): proactively searches systems for threats that evade automated defenses, testing hypotheses against telemetry.
- [Malware Analyst](../roles/defensive-security-roles/malware-analyst.md): studies malicious software to learn how it works so defenders can detect, contain, and remediate it.
- [Incident Responder](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/incident-responder.md): investigates active and suspected breaches, limits their damage, and restores affected systems to a trusted state.

## Skills and certifications

- **System and network models:** recognizing malicious activity depends first on knowing what normal looks like, so operating system internals, networking fundamentals, and logging and telemetry are foundational.
- **Attacker awareness:** familiarity with common techniques as cataloged in [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/).
- **Tooling:** comfort with SIEM platforms, endpoint detection systems, and network sensors, plus the query skills to correlate events.
- **Analytical temperament:** forming a hypothesis, following evidence, and staying calm during a live incident.
- **Communication and credentials:** explaining what happened and what to do next to engineers, managers, and executives under time pressure, supported by foundational certifications and hands-on lab practice.

## How to progress

People commonly enter through help desk, system administration, or network roles that build operational grounding, and many start as tier-one analysts in a security operations center. From there the path branches: an analyst may deepen into incident handling, grow into detection engineering and threat hunting, move toward security engineering that builds defensive tooling, or step into leadership that runs the operations function. Experience with real incidents is what distinguishes senior practitioners. Defensive security consumes the findings offensive security produces, draws on threat intelligence to prioritize what to watch for, and hands the deepest cases to specialized DFIR practitioners when an incident demands forensic depth.
