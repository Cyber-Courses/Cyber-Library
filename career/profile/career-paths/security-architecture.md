# Security Architecture

**Security architecture** is the discipline of designing how systems, networks, and controls fit together so protection is part of their structure rather than something bolted on later. An architect works at the level of blueprints and standards, deciding how **trust boundaries**, identity, segmentation, encryption, and monitoring should be arranged across an environment. The goal is to make secure choices the default, so that teams building and running systems inherit sound protection without reinventing it each time. Because these decisions shape an organization for years, the role rewards people who think in terms of structure, trade-offs, and long horizons.

## Roles on this path

- [Security Engineer](../roles/security-architecture-roles/security-engineer.md): designs, builds, and maintains the technical security controls that protect an organization's systems and infrastructure.
- [Security Architect](../roles/security-architecture-roles/security-architect.md): turns business and risk requirements into the structural security design of systems, platforms, and controls.
- [Domain Security Architect](../roles/security-architecture-roles/domain-security-architect.md): owns the architecture and controls for a specific business, technology, or functional domain.
- [Enterprise Security Architect](../roles/security-architecture-roles/enterprise-security-architect.md): defines and governs the security architecture spanning the whole organization, aligning controls with strategy and risk.
- [Solutions Architect for Security](../roles/security-architecture-roles/solutions-architect-security.md): embeds requirements and controls into the design of a particular product, platform, or business solution.

## Skills and certifications

- **Technical breadth:** a working understanding of networks, operating systems, applications, identity, and increasingly cloud platforms, with the judgment to see how they interact.
- **Frameworks and catalogs:** familiarity with recognized frameworks and control catalogs that give designs a common language.
- **Threat modeling:** reasoning about the threats that matter to a proposed system and defining the controls and reference architectures that should apply.
- **Communication:** clear writing, diagramming, and the ability to hold a position in a design debate while staying open to constraints.
- **Credentials:** senior certifications such as those tied to the [CISSP and ISC2 architecture resources](https://www.isc2.org/) are common markers of the breadth the role expects.

## How to progress

Security architecture is rarely a first job. Most architects arrive after years in a hands-on domain such as security engineering, network or systems engineering, software development, or defensive operations, where they built practical knowledge of how systems behave and fail. From there, practitioners move into senior or principal architect roles, specialize in a domain such as cloud or application architecture, or progress toward enterprise architecture and strategic leadership. The role builds on the hands-on depth of application and cloud security, is informed by the controls and obligations governance and risk work defines, and depends on identity and access design as one of its central building blocks.
