# DFIR

**DFIR**, short for **digital forensics and incident response**, is the specialization concerned with what happens after something goes wrong. It joins two related disciplines: **incident response**, the coordinated effort to contain and recover from an active event, and **digital forensics**, the rigorous examination of systems and data to reconstruct what took place. Practitioners investigate suspected or confirmed incidents, determine how an intrusion occurred and how far it spread, and guide the organization back to a trusted state, turning a confusing, high-pressure situation into a clear account of events that supports both recovery and accountability.

## Roles on this path

- [Incident Responder](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/incident-responder.md): investigates active and suspected breaches, limits their damage, and restores affected systems to a trusted state.
- [Digital Forensics Analyst](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/digital-forensics-analyst.md): recovers, preserves, and analyzes digital evidence to reconstruct what happened during incidents, misuse, and disputes.
- [Malware Reverse Engineer](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/malware-reverse-engineer.md): analyzes malicious software to understand its behavior, origin, and impact, producing intelligence that drives detection and response.
- [Forensics Manager](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/forensics-manager.md): leads the people, process, and casework of a DFIR function so investigations stay defensible and timely.
- [eDiscovery Specialist](../roles/forensics-and-ir-roles/ediscovery-specialist.md): identifies, preserves, collects, and produces electronically stored information so it can be reviewed defensibly in litigation and investigations.

## Skills and certifications

- **System internals:** operating system internals, file systems, memory structures, networking, and common log sources.
- **Attacker movement:** how adversaries move through an environment once they gain a foothold.
- **Acquisition and analysis:** familiarity with forensic acquisition and analysis tooling for disk and memory images.
- **Disciplined reasoning:** forming hypotheses, testing them against evidence, and resisting premature conclusions.
- **Evidence and communication:** a documented chain of custody for cases that may reach legal or regulatory action, plus calm briefing of both engineers and executives; response practice follows guidance such as [NIST Special Publication 800-61 Revision 3](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/61/r3/final).

## How to progress

People commonly enter from a security operations center, from systems or network administration, or from a general incident response team, bringing familiarity with how normal environments behave so abnormal activity stands out. Many build credibility through hands-on practice, structured study, and recognized certifications before leading investigations. With experience, a practitioner grows into senior investigator or incident commander roles, specializes in areas such as malware analysis, memory forensics, or cloud and mobile forensics, or moves into threat hunting and detection engineering. Because DFIR is inherently reactive, its long-term influence comes from closing the loop: a mature practice extracts durable lessons that improve monitoring, response procedures, and resilience over time.
