# Principles

Good security rests on a handful of durable ideas. Technologies change, threats evolve, but a small set of principles continues to guide how systems should be designed, defended, and operated. This category gathers those principles so readers have a shared vocabulary for everything that follows. It anchors the Foundation pillar because these concepts underpin nearly every control, policy, and decision in the field.

The topics below define the essentials.

**The CIA triad** describes the three goals of confidentiality, integrity, and availability that most controls serve.

**Defense in depth** layers protections so that no single failure exposes the whole system.

**Least privilege** grants each user and process only the access it genuinely needs.

**Zero trust** assumes no implicit trust and verifies every request.

**Accountability** ensures actions can be traced to a responsible party.

**Authentication and authorization** establish who someone is and what they are allowed to do.

**Security by design** builds protection in from the start rather than bolting it on later.

**Minimization** reduces the data, access, and attack surface to only what is required.
